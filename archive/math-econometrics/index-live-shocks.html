What is the line drawn behind this page?+

It is a single trajectory of a small model of a market maker, computed in your browser while you read. A market maker posts a bid and an ask around the current price and earns the spread, but every fill changes its inventory, and holding inventory is a risk. Guéant, Lehalle and Fernandez-Tapia solved the resulting control problem: the optimal distance of each quote from the price is a closed formula in the risk aversion, the volatility, and how quickly fills die off as you quote further away. The model uses those formulas as the maker's quotes.

The twist is that the market here reacts to the maker. Three quantities evolve together: the inventory q, the imbalance I between bid and ask volume in the order book, and a micro-price adjustment D that follows the imbalance with a lag τ. A fill on the bid side moves the inventory and pushes the imbalance, the imbalance moves the micro-price, the micro-price shifts the quotes, and the quotes change the fill rates. A small periodic flow from outside keeps the whole thing from ever quite sitting still.

The lag τ is what scrolling controls. At the top of the page it is short: the micro-price reacts almost instantly, the loop overshoots, and the trajectory winds around a torus without ever repeating itself. Scrolling down makes the lag longer. Around τ ≈ 0.37 the loop passes a Hopf bifurcation and the torus collapses onto a stable equilibrium. From there on, every so often a burst of one-sided orders knocks the state off that equilibrium, and you can watch the maker work the shock off in a slowly decaying spiral, hatched by the outside flow. Scroll back up and the torus unwinds again.

What you see is the three-dimensional path (q, I, D) projected onto the screen and rotated slowly with the scroll position. It is drawn as one line that fades with age, so the figure is always an actual solution of the equations, also while τ is changing. On wide screens the equations and the current τ are printed in the bottom-right corner.

Guéant, Lehalle, Fernandez-Tapia (2013), Dealing with the inventory risk: a solution to the market making problem, Math. Financ. Econ. · Avellaneda, Stoikov (2008), High-frequency trading in a limit order book, Quant. Finance.
